The operator causes the match; the film implies neither automatic reconciliation nor measured savings.

- **Follow the record, recommended.** Begin in the authentic table, isolate one invoice, show the supported matching action and its result, then inspect a separate unmatched item. Persistent identifiers supply credibility. Weakness: small UI needs careful cropping and sufficient holds.

# Case 2: Scribbleclub

Recommend **a duet of marks that becomes one composition**. The visual argument: two visibly distinct people sketch concurrently, then their selected shapes appear together on one shared board. The product value is shared authorship, not speed or an AI-generated result.

Three treatments:

- **Duet on one canvas, recommended.** Coral and ink strokes develop simultaneously in different areas, then selected shapes form a shared composition. The marks themselves carry the story. Weakness: overlapping activity needs clear ownership and a controlled attention hierarchy.
- **Two viewpoints, one board.** Split the frame into each person's working view, then reveal the same shared board occupying the full frame. This emphasizes simultaneity and common context. Weakness: split-screen reduces legibility and must not imply unsupported synchronization details.
- **A joint poster takes shape.** Begin with an incomplete illustrative composition; each person's contribution fills a different compositional need, with the full board revealed at the end. This offers stronger emotional payoff but can suggest automatic layout unless human selection and assembly stay clear.

Keep the film warm and expressive: large hand-drawn marks, coral and ink, generous space. Motion verbs: draw, answer, gather, rest. Slight irregularity belongs to marks and expressive graphics; product labels remain stable. Avoid perpetual wiggle. The quiet phrase is an opportunity to watch and read, not a gap to fill with extra cuts.

**Provisional 24-second script and energy curve:** 0–4s, “Two people.” with two initial marks; 4–10s, “Sketch together.” during simultaneous drawing; 10–20s, “Choose what belongs.” while selected shapes settle into view through the quiet phrase; 20–24s, “One shared board. Scribbleclub.” at the final accent and held aftermath. These are editorial time budgets, not measured musical events. Confirm the selected track excerpt accommodates the quiet phrase, final accent and closing hold within 24 seconds before assigning exact frames.

**Detailed shot, approximately 4–10s:** A large canvas dominates. Two named authors, presented as film annotations rather than invented product badges, establish ownership. One creates an angular ink shape while another creates a coral curved shape; their active intervals overlap visibly. The first mark anchors attention, the second begins before it finishes, and both then stop. Hold their completed work for roughly two seconds. The consequential change is visible simultaneous contribution. Both shapes survive into the selection/assembly passage, establishing authorship continuity. Use an explicitly illustrative canvas until authentic UI is supplied; do not invent toolbars, buttons or cursor behaviors and call them product footage.

**Smallest useful checkpoint:** one rough continuous 0–22s clip, ending after the shared-board reveal and its first reading hold. This longer checkpoint is justified because the quiet phrase and final accent carry the assembly payoff; a polished opening alone cannot test it. Use simple authored marks and the intended track, without finishing the closing brand frame. Measure the track and validate its grid before production; retain action-led drawing and reading holds as named exceptions to snapping. Judge mute comprehension separately from normal-speed playback and listening. No musical feel has been verified here.
