## Case 2: collaboration-play

**Recommend “two gestures become one board”: let the viewer see two people making different marks simultaneously, then combine their selected shapes.** The proof is the interaction itself. There is no speed claim or promise of better design.

Three treatments:

- **Shared canvas, recommended.** Two cursor gestures develop in one continuous field. Their shapes remain recognizable after combining. The quiet musical phrase gives the collaboration room to read; the final accent pays it off.
- **Paper duet.** Two separate paper-like panels carry distinct drawing styles, then selected shapes travel into a shared board. Human warmth comes from the marks and their contrast, rather than realistic desk footage.
- **Result first.** Open on the combined board, separate it into its two contributors’ shapes, then replay the simultaneous sketching and combine action. This puts the outcome before the process; the separation is an editorial device, not an undo feature claim.

**Provisional 24-second plan:**

- **0–4s:** Two already-visible marks, with draft display copy “Sketch together.” Coral and ink distinguish contributions without adding a competing palette.
- **4–10s:** Two people sketch at the same time. Show a clear interval where both contributions advance, rather than alternating isolated cursor actions.
- **10–20s:** Stay with the canvas through the quiet phrase. Let the viewer recognize the two sets of shapes; select the shapes that will combine. Keep most beats empty. Draft copy: “Bring your shapes together.”
- **20–24s:** The combine result lands on the track’s final accent, followed by the Scribbleclub name. The precise accent and available reading hold must be measured before these provisional boundaries are finalized.

**Detailed shot, the quiet phrase into the accent:** Open with both contributors’ shapes present, not an empty canvas. One coral line grows while an ink shape closes beside it; their motions overlap to establish simultaneity. Both marks settle. A restrained selection treatment identifies the shapes being combined. Keep the rest of the canvas still through the musical quiet. On the accent, the selected shapes arrive together in the shared board, retaining their geometry and colors so the result is causally readable. Do not auto-clean, beautify, or generate additional drawing. Let the result hold instead of adding a celebratory confetti layer.

Use a continuous selected track passage that includes the quiet-to-accent relationship so the review tests the film’s central move. Show the complete 24-second script alongside it. Review recognition muted, then musical fit by watching and listening; stills alone cannot establish either rhythm or smoothness.

## Contradictions and tensions encountered in the frozen skill

- `SKILL.md` says every cut lands on a backbeat. `beat-sync.md` calls backbeat placement a strong recommendation and documents accepted downbeat cuts. For planning, I treated it as a default governed by the actual track and locked-cut constraints.
- `SKILL.md` says to make sensible defaults when the user has no preference and to proceed without document sign-offs. `reference-video-vibe.md` explicitly says not to skip reference selection when the user says “I trust you,” and asks for at least one reference or a starter-set choice. I completed the requested plans, marked reference-derived motion as unverified, and left reference analysis as a production input.
- `asset-intake.md` says there is no soundtrack fallback for the minimum viable production set. Case 1 explicitly has no soundtrack and permits planning only. I supplied provisional reading budgets without claiming measured beat-sync or sourcing a track.
- The main skill says to stop exactly once before the full build. `script-development.md` describes locking the macro shape and approving the screen-by-screen script before the brief. I used the main skill’s clip-plus-script checkpoint instead of creating a separate document approval gate.
- The revision case’s differing IDs conflict with a truthful same-record shared-element handoff while all copy is locked. That is an input constraint, not a skill contradiction. The proposal avoids implying one record changes into the other and makes the prerequisite explicit if same-record continuity is required.
