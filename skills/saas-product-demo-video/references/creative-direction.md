# Creative direction

Read for a new film, a genuinely open direction, or a request to rethink generic work. A local timing or copy fix does not require this process.

## Make a visual argument

Identify the viewer, the useful change in their understanding, the product evidence supporting it and the emotional register. Then write:

> The viewer sees [specific object or relationship] change from [state] to [state], making [supported product value] understandable.

"""Cinematic, premium, Apple-like""" leaves the decision unmade. """An unmatched payment stays in view while its corresponding invoice is found and the two become a reconciled row""" provides an observable event. It is valid only if the product supports that relationship. Fictional sample data must be identified and internally consistent.

Find the product's useful verbs: reconcile, assemble, compare, route, trace, invite, edit. Choose an action the viewer benefits from seeing. A product can own a behavior more convincingly than a generic glow or particle field.

## Explore before the components pick the idea

Draft two or three compact treatments before building full scenes. Each names a mechanism, representative composition, decisive transformation, evidence and weakness. Keep the approved audience, claims, brand and deliverables constant.

Illustrative alternatives for a reconciliation product:

| Treatment | Mechanism | Useful when | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| Follow one item | An actual row persists through selection, context and resolution | The workflow is the strongest proof | Less useful when the real interaction is visually opaque |
| Make agreement visible | Two columns align through shared identifiers; an unmatched item stays apart | Matching is the core capability | Can imply automatic accuracy unless the UI proves it |
| Reveal the exception | A dense authentic overview becomes a clear exception and next action | Prioritization matters | Density needs enough time to establish context |

These are examples, not templates for every product. A collaborative drawing tool might use marks responding to other marks; an API tool might show a request becoming inspectable output. Objects, spatial logic, timing and emotional register should follow the product.

Reject a treatment if it needs invented functionality, cannot survive muted viewing, or exceeds available assets and time. Recommend a treatment with a reason and tradeoff. A second render is useful only when the difference is difficult to judge on paper.

## Establish a direction the build can follow

Put this compact agreement in the project brief, not in the reusable skill:

- **Visual argument:** one sentence, with supporting product evidence.
- **Visual world:** composition, typography, color, material and depth choices, with their role in the idea.
- **Motion behavior:** a few verbs and their roles; include how objects settle and when the frame becomes still.
- **Signature moment:** one memorable transformation or reveal, if needed, with its setup and payoff.
- **Limits and exceptions:** scope, time, assets, approved decisions and intentional departures from the vocabulary.

The style should be convincing in a plain frame before animation covers it. Check hierarchy, silhouette, optical alignment, text size and empty space at intended playback size. A small UI label should not carry the entire claim.

## What reads as a template

A film can use every hex from the brand file and still read as one more AI-made reel. Owners name the look in one word ("claudesque", "Canva", "cheap"), and it is a set of defaults, not an accident. The list, each one seen rejected on a production:

- A cream or off-white canvas with a serif headline on it, soft radial glows, drifting blurred blobs, grain or light drift that bands on dark fields.
- One italic, cursive or colored word in a headline; gradient text; a mono "technical" eyebrow label inside cards (SPONSOR, ENTITY); tag pills everywhere; three equal icon cards; same-size card rows.
- A hub-and-spoke node diagram for any relationship; clip-art glyphs (a rectangle with dot windows for a building, a generic avatar); gray placeholder bars standing in for text ("admits it is a mock").
- An invented dashboard when the real product's screens are on its site. This one alone sank a whole version.
- Pop-culture sample names (the fictional paper company); stock footage of a frustrated person; a demo-reel of effects, one trick per scene; something popping on every beat; idle bobbing on held elements; text that fades in and sits; frames that are empty on the cut and fill over the next beats; antithesis slogans.

What replaced each of them on the film that shipped: an ink field with white cards as objects and the brand's own panels; the real UI rebuilt from the site and measured from the DOM; the site's own sample universe; entities drawn in the film's established language (an axonometric property built from the map's dots, not a building icon); one text move for the whole film; cuts on backbeats with most beats empty; sample text that reads as real, logged as sample data. The palette is necessary, not sufficient: judge the whole frame.

One signature move per film. Everything else is entrances, holds, cuts and the camera. Three set pieces is noise; the reference films that look expensive each have one, and earn it with a three-stage event.

## Reference by reason

Use references to answer a specific uncertainty: preserving identity, making collaboration visible, introducing expressive type or sustaining a reading hold. Borrow the principle and reconstruct it in the product's language. Do not combine the union of every admired transition.

If the user supplied a previous film, determine what they value: pacing, clarity, tone or a moment. Preserve what they approved for this project. Another product need not inherit every cut or color. When trusted to choose, use available evidence and the atlas; do not force the user to find references.

## Spend effort where it changes the film

For a bounded pass, investigate the weakest important sequence and prototype it with adjacent context. For a new film, build the hard transformation and one truthful product moment first. Detail, texture and sound polish follow the chosen composition and timing.

If removing a camera move, texture or SFX leaves the same understanding and a cleaner frame, prefer the simpler version unless the expressive contribution is deliberate and useful. Restraint and exuberance can both be appropriate.

Completion: the brief names a recommended direction, rejected alternatives, supporting evidence and the cheapest meaningful test. This feeds the existing render checkpoint, not another approval ceremony.
