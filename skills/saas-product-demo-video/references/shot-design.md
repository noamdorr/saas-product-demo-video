# Shot design: meaning through time

Use when outlining scenes, redesigning a product sequence or briefing a scene builder. Each important shot should change what the viewer knows.

## A shot contract

| Field | What to specify |
|---|---|
| Viewer takeaway | The one idea the viewer should retain |
| Evidence and visual mode | Screenshot, faithful reconstruction or illustrative abstraction; source of any claim |
| Starting attention | Where the eye starts and why it has priority |
| Meaningful object | The row, mark, document, action or relationship carrying the idea |
| Change and consequence | What changes, what caused it and what becomes understandable |
| Reading hold | When meaning is fully visible, how long it remains readable, what stops moving |
| Handoff | What survives into the next shot, or why a deliberate break is useful |

Keep exact copy and event frames in the brief and central timing file. The contract explains their purpose without duplicating implementation constants.

## Direct attention

Choose one dominant event at a time. Arrival, a new headline, a cursor click and a result should not compete for the same instant unless their grouping is unmistakable. Move supporting events earlier, later or less. Keep the new focal point near the previous one when continuity matters; a deliberate relocation can signal a chapter change.

Work across scales: context establishes credibility, a crop makes the action legible, a consequence proves why it mattered. Keep one or two recognizable UI landmarks while suppressing irrelevant detail. A reconstruction may simplify layout, but must preserve meaning and interaction sequence. Do not require a complete desktop screen to be read in a phone-sized player.

A composed cut frame can be an object, title, deliberate empty field or transition surface. Its purpose must be clear in playback. Avoid accidental dead frames while waiting for content. Pre-roll an entrance only when it preserves truthful state; meaningful metrics must not pass through fictitious values merely to keep the frame busy.

## Make transitions carry something

| Relationship | Useful device | Check |
|---|---|---|
| Same object, new context | Shared position, label or shape | Is it really the same record? |
| Action produces result | Input, response, settled consequence | Does the product produce that result? |
| Part belongs to whole | Pull back or reveal context | Is the relationship readable? |
| Before and after | Controlled replacement or split | Are the states comparable and truthful? |
| New chapter or deliberate contrast | Hard cut, silence, color or scale break | Would continuity falsely imply a relationship? |

A match cut between different sample IDs can imply false provenance even when geometrically perfect. Use a neutral chapter cut, label the change of example, or obtain a coherent approved example. Never silently rewrite IDs to connect unrelated real records.

Expressive punctuation is legitimate; name its rhythmic or emotional role rather than inventing a product explanation. Repeated transitions lose surprise. Reserve a conspicuous one for a moment that deserves attention.

## Worked example, fictional data

Claim: an operator can inspect why a build failed. Supplied UI shows job `B-42`, its failed step and corresponding log.

- **Shot:** keep `B-42` visible as its queue row expands into detail context. Reduce competing jobs' prominence and preserve the label through the handoff.
- **Change:** selecting the actual failed step reveals its log line. Input and response occur in sequence.
- **Hold:** camera and log stop while the line is readable. State the verified capability, not an invented resolution time.
- **Next:** widen to the real retry control only if retry is supported and relevant. Otherwise end on understanding the cause.
- **Comparator:** a clean cut from selected row to already framed detail. Keep it if the morph makes the relationship harder to read.

## Develop the sequence

Check the contact sheet for repeated scale, composition and narrative role. Repetition can establish a pattern, but change it when the story changes. Reveal a dependency, follow an object, show input/result or let a decisive detail fill the frame. Choose based on meaning, not a layout-variety quota.

Completion: the action and consequence can be understood from the shot without an explanation outside the film. Verify in playback when possible; stills establish only composition and sampled states.
