# Motion language and rhythm

Read after selecting a direction and before choosing curves or timing. Build a coherent performance whose emphasis follows meaning.

## Give motion roles

| Role | Decision |
|---|---|
| Orient | How context arrives and where the eye settles |
| Operate | How selection, input and response behave like the product |
| Emphasize | What deserves a distinct accent and how rarely it occurs |
| Connect | What stays recognizable between states or shots |
| Resolve | How the result settles and gives the viewer time to understand |

Roles may share a primitive or need exceptions. One spring everywhere is an implementation shortcut, not a direction. Test acceleration, travel, overlap and settling for the intended weight and responsiveness. Pattern-library values are starting points.

## Author contrast

Sketch an energy curve in the brief. Mark context, acceleration, key detail and release. Vary scale, density, speed and duration where the argument needs emphasis. A quiet hold can make the next move stronger; density can establish volume before selection.

Do not add idle motion to every hold. A stopped camera and settled text often improve comprehension. Use drift when it changes emphasis, shows depth or maintains intentional travel. Check the late hold and handoff, not only the entrance.

## Sound and visual timing

Choose the pacing authority: music, action, narration or intentional silence. A soundtrack may support an action-led sequence without dictating each cut. Use `beat-sync.md` for grids, trims and measured events.

For music-led work, identify phrase changes and a few decisive accents before snapping events. Anticipation can precede a hit, the meaningful landing can coincide with it, and settling can follow. Leave room between accents. Downbeat, offbeat and phrase-crossing cuts can be intentional. Record the choice centrally so alignment tools preserve it.

Evaluate at normal speed. Half-speed viewing diagnoses defects but changes the experience. Audio measurements establish onset and level, not whether a mix feels good. Silent viewing tests whether the argument is visible.

## Typography has roles

Display type can be expressive; UI labels should remain credible and legible. Use consistent treatments by role: stable explanation, brief emphasis, a closing gesture. A word-mask reveal is one option. A hard appearance, restrained dissolve or held phrase can be better when the image already moves.

Fit the longest actual line before tuning its entrance. Allow reading time after the words settle. Character animation earns its cost when writing is meaningful; otherwise it delays the message. The template tells stay banned whatever the brand does on its site: one italic or colored word in a headline, gradient text, an eyebrow label over every heading, a serif on cream for its own sake (`creative-direction.md`, "What reads as a template"). A brand's own display face, its serif, its color are the material; the tells are how that material is misused.

## One region of change at a time

The eye follows change. Two changes in far-apart regions of the frame within about 0.4 s split it: a date counter spinning at the top while the number it describes lands at the bottom; a column sliding on the left while a question types on the right; tags landing on one side while a pin turns red on the other. Each was found in an attention pass over a finished film and each was fixed the same way: put the secondary element where the eye already is (the counter beside the number), or have it present on the cut, or let it wait for the main event. A camera move is exempt; it moves the whole frame together. Accented elements at a scene change are fine, because the cut resets the eye.

Reading time is set inside the scene before it is set by its length. A tile legible for 0.17 s before the picture moved on was "not enough time to read"; a whole extra beat of hold (1.1 s) was "too slow"; 0.9 s, taken by raising the words earlier and starting the next move half a beat later, was right. Lengthen the hold only when the scene has nothing to give.

## Depth, material and finish

Use depth to explain hierarchy, context or a viewpoint change. Limit perspective by UI readability in actual frames. Texture should survive export without shimmer or competition with fine labels. Flat graphic precision is equally valid.

For shared elements, match identity first, geometry second and velocity where needed. A deliberate editorial cut can reset velocity. Physical continuity is not compulsory at every boundary.

Keep rendering deterministic: values derive from frame and stable inputs. Prototype a new mechanism locally before adding another animation runtime. Official Remotion guidance owns API details; borrowing ideas from other tools does not require their dependencies.

Completion: quiet and emphasis are intentional, typography has assigned roles, and conspicuous motion has a communicative or expressive purpose assessable in the render.
