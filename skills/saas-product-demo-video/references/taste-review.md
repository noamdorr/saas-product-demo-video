# Taste review

Use after a checkpoint, material revision or full candidate render. Technical checks remain in `self-check.md`; this review asks whether the idea works.

## Declare evidence

Name the exact render and version. Record intervals inspected, normal-speed playback, audio listening and viewing size. Read the latest approvals so a source experiment is not mistaken for the accepted film.

Stills establish sampled composition. Frame strips suggest continuity. Measurements locate changes and audio onsets. None alone establishes complete motion or listening quality. Model video analysis is useful evidence with its method disclosed, not human acceptance.

## Judge five dimensions

| Dimension | Question | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Specificity | Could an unrelated product use this unchanged except for its logo? | Product-specific action, object or relationship |
| Clarity | What changed, why did it matter, and was it readable? | Focal event, reading interval, muted viewing |
| Continuity | Does the handoff preserve intended meaning? | Matching identities, cause/result, justified breaks |
| Contrast | Does emphasis emerge from quieter or simpler moments? | Scale, density, speed and duration across shots |
| Finish | Does execution support the idea without distracting defects? | Optical spacing, masks, curves, credible UI and reviewed sound |

Give evidence and consequences rather than unsupported numerical taste scores. A beautiful frame can fail as a sequence; a plain frame can be right for a complicated idea.

## Diagnose before prescribing effects

- Repeated panels may need a developing argument, not more camera travel.
- Boring text may mean weak copy, repeated hierarchy, poor reading contrast or mismatched motion. Test the cause before changing every reveal.
- Jerkiness may come from competing events, hard landings, a mismatched cut, grid alignment or a render defect. Inspect the interval.
- Premium may mean fewer distractions, better composition, material character or a memorable idea. Anchor the interpretation in the brief and visible evidence.
- An effective abstract shot may still fail to establish what the product does. Return to authentic UI where credibility is missing.

## Compare a real choice

Use the accepted cut or simpler treatment as comparator. Keep assets, claims, copy, duration and music constant where possible; label necessary differences. Review full-size candidates as well as side-by-side because small UI details disappear at half width.

For a direction, compare the uncertain sequence with adjacent context, commonly 8–12 seconds. This is a practical budget, not a rule. A local fix may need less. Do not build several full films to answer one question.

When a viewer is available, ask what they understood and remember before naming the intended effect. Optional blind A/B labels can reduce novelty bias. Keep the simpler or earlier version when the extra work does not improve the result.

## Return an actionable review

Rank up to three material findings, each with timestamp or frame, symptom, consequence, proposed change, effort and a useful comparison. Separate observed defects from interpretations and untested hypotheses.

Critique-only requests stop at review. Authorized bounded fixes proceed through verification. A change of visual direction gets the smallest useful test and one taste decision unless that choice was already delegated.

Completion: name what works, the remaining material issue and any missing evidence. A film is not accepted merely because its author prefers it, a detector is quiet or a render completed.
